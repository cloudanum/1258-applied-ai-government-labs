# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
Sorting by the underlying job, not by surface keywords, is what makes each placement defensible. Every sticky carries a signal verb: *find* (E1) is dataset discovery, *label* (E2) is classification, *group without labels* (E3) is clustering, *predict over time* (E4) is regression/forecasting, *answer from a fixed document* (E5) is grounded QA, *flag unusual* (E6) is anomaly detection, *extract names and dates* (E7) is information extraction, and *rewrite* (E8) is GenAI transformation. Naming the pattern matters more than naming a tool because the pattern fixes what data you need, how you will evaluate the system, and its most likely failure mode, all before any vendor enters the conversation. E5 and E8 both involve language models, which is exactly the trap: one grounds its answer in a fixed source and the other produces new language, and confusing them means buying a generator when you needed a retriever. The weaker instinct, "it is a chatbot, so GenAI," fails on E5 because grounded QA is evaluated against a source document, not on fluency.

## A complete solution
Full placement: E1 dataset discovery/search, E2 classification, E3 clustering, E4 regression/forecasting, E5 grounded QA (RAG-like), E6 anomaly detection, E7 information extraction/NER, E8 GenAI transformation. Posted remotely, the chat line is `E1-discovery, E2-classification, E3-clustering, E4-regression, E5-grounded-QA, E6-anomaly, E7-extraction, E8-transformation`.

Take E5, "A chatbot answers only from the current policy PDF," the one most people hesitate on. The wrong instinct is to shout GenAI because it is a chatbot that writes sentences. The correct defense sounds like this: "The chatbot interface is surface detail. The job is answering, and the answer is constrained to one fixed, approved document. That is grounded QA: retrieval plus generation with a citation requirement, and the test is whether every claim traces back to the PDF. The failure mode is inventing plausible-sounding policy, which is why the words 'only from' are the whole design. E8 is the mirror image: it takes known text and produces new language at a grade 6 reading level, so nothing needs grounding and the failure mode is drifting from the source meaning. Same model family underneath, different pattern, different evaluation plan." That comment earns the point because it names the signal (the fixed source), the pattern, and the failure mode. The disagreement worth listening for in step 5 is E5 versus E8: if a neighbor placed both under GenAI, ask them how they would test each one, and the difference surfaces on its own.
