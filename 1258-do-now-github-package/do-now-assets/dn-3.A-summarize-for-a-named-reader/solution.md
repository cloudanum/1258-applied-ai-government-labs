# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
The facts are fixed; only the framing moves, and naming the reader first forces the keep, cut, and emphasis decisions a reader-less "summarize this" never makes. The same one-line instruction is also the cheapest upgrade to any summarization prompt.

## A complete solution
Working from the source paragraph, "The department will pilot a triage assistant for routine inquiries in Q3. The pilot excludes benefits decisions and keeps human review for escalations," two complete posts:

`READER: EXECUTIVE | SUMMARY: The Q3 triage pilot is low-risk by design: it covers routine inquiries only, excludes benefits decisions, and keeps human review on every escalation. I am asking for your approval to launch in Q3 on those guardrails.`

`READER: CITIZEN | SUMMARY: Starting in Q3, an automated assistant may answer your routine questions when you contact the department. It will not make any decision about your benefits, and a person will review your case whenever it is escalated.`

Both versions keep all four facts (Q3 timing, routine inquiries only, benefits excluded, human review of escalations) but spend them differently: the executive version turns the exclusions into risk posture and adds a decision ask, while the citizen version puts "triage assistant" into plain words and leads with the reader's own experience. The swap to notice in the other reader's post: the executive keeps the word "pilot" because it signals limited scope, while the citizen version cuts it because it signals nothing to a resident.
