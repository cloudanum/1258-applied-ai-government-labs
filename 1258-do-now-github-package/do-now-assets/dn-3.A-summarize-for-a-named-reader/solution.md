# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
The facts are fixed; only the framing moves. Naming the reader first forces the keep, cut, and emphasis decisions that a reader-less "summarize this" never makes: an executive needs the risk posture and what must be decided, a citizen needs to know what changes for them and what stays human. The two-sentence limit is doing real work too, because it makes under-inclusion impossible to hide behind length. The same instruction is also the single cheapest upgrade to any summarization prompt: adding "for a deputy minister" or "for a resident with no policy background" changes the output more than any other one-line edit. A long, audience-free summary fails both readers at once: the executive cannot find the ask, and the citizen cannot find themselves in it.

## A complete solution
Working from the source paragraph, "The department will pilot a triage assistant for routine inquiries in Q3. The pilot excludes benefits decisions and keeps human review for escalations," two complete posts:

`READER: EXECUTIVE | SUMMARY: The Q3 triage pilot is low-risk by design: it covers routine inquiries only, excludes benefits decisions, and keeps human review on every escalation. I am asking for your approval to launch in Q3 on those guardrails.`

`READER: CITIZEN | SUMMARY: Starting in Q3, an automated assistant may answer your routine questions when you contact the department. It will not make any decision about your benefits, and a person will review your case whenever it is escalated.`

Both versions keep all four facts (Q3 timing, routine inquiries only, benefits excluded, human review of escalations) but spend them differently. The executive version translates the exclusions into risk posture and adds the one thing the source implies but never states, a decision ask; the citizen version translates "triage assistant" into plain words and leads with the reader's own experience. Reading a classmate's post for the other reader, the swap to notice is that the executive keeps the word "pilot" because it signals limited scope, while the citizen version cuts it because it signals nothing to a resident. The classic failure to avoid is the blended version: the source paragraph rewritten slightly shorter, which serves nobody and is what an unnamed "summarize this" reliably produces.
