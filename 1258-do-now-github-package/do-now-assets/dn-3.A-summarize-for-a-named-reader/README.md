# Do Now 3.A: Summarize for a Named Reader 🎯

**Time:** 5 minutes
**Format:** No sandbox or tooling required, this activity is done mentally (writing the two sentences yourself) and captured in Zoom chat (in-room participants may add their summary to the Mural board instead).
**Mural:** https://app.mural.co/t/day10626/m/day10626/1790240791066/a202e3520b49f45cb3ce0dacb8edd98d6be5caba

## Goal
By the end of this five-minute exercise you will have taken one short instructor-provided paragraph, "The department will pilot a triage assistant for routine inquiries in Q3. The pilot excludes benefits decisions and keeps human review for escalations.", and rewritten it as a two-sentence summary tuned to one named reader: an executive (who needs risk posture and a decision ask) or a citizen (who needs to know what changes for them and what does not). The artifact is your reader label plus your two sentences, posted in the capture format `READER: ___ | SUMMARY: ___`. The skill underneath is audience-first framing: the facts stay fixed, but what you keep, cut, and emphasize must change with the reader. You will leave with the instinct to name the reader before writing any summary, by hand or in a prompt, because "summarize this" without an audience produces mush.

## Why it matters
Tuning a summary to a specific reader is a core AI-at-work habit, and the same instruction ("name the reader") is the single cheapest upgrade to any summarization prompt. Doing it in chat makes the reasoning visible and easy to correct, so the room can see how one paragraph becomes two very different documents.

**Assets:** Short instructor-provided paragraph (see `board-items.tsv` for the reader/output table).

**🌐 MS Co-pilot Specific Info:** Paste the paragraph into Copilot with the reader named in the prompt (“for a deputy minister” / “for a resident with no policy background”) and compare its draft with yours, where did it over- or under-simplify?

## Run steps 🪜
1. Read the source paragraph carefully, the triage-assistant pilot above, and list its facts: Q3 timing, routine inquiries only, benefits decisions excluded, human review on escalations.
2. Pick your named reader from the table in `board-items.tsv`: **Executive** (two sentences: risk posture and decision ask) or **Citizen** (two sentences: what changes for me and what does not), or counsel/ops if the instructor offers them.
3. Write exactly two sentences for that reader. Every word must serve them: executives get the guardrails and what you need decided; citizens get what changes in their experience and what stays human.
4. Post your work in Zoom chat using the capture template: `READER: ___ | SUMMARY: ___`. In the room without chat? Add a Mural sticky in the same format on the board linked above.
5. Read a post from someone who chose the *other* reader, name one fact they kept that you cut, and one they cut that you kept.
6. If the instructor runs it with AI, ask your approved assistant for the same two-reader summaries and compare: did naming the reader visibly change its output? Say where in one chat reply.

## Key takeaway 💡
Naming the reader before you summarize, in your own head or in a prompt, is what turns a generic summary into a useful one: same facts, different decisions about what to keep.

## Common mistake to name ⚠️
Writing a long, reader-less summary with no stated audience or required format, "summarize this" thinking. Require the reader label and the two-sentence limit before anyone posts.

## If finished early ⏩
Rewrite your summary for the opposite reader (executive ↔ citizen) without changing a single fact, feel which words have to move, and note the hardest swap in one chat line.

## ⭐ Bonus (optional)
Do a third pass for a new reader, agency counsel, in two sentences: what is legally safe about this pilot and what needs review before launch. Post `READER: COUNSEL | SUMMARY: ...` and notice how the same paragraph has now produced three different documents.
