# Do Now 3.A: Summarize for a Named Reader 🎯

**Time:** 5 minutes
**Format:** No sandbox or tooling required, this activity is done mentally (writing the two sentences yourself) and captured in Zoom chat (in-room participants may add their summary to the Mural board instead).
**Mural:** https://app.mural.co/t/day10626/m/day10626/1790240791066/a202e3520b49f45cb3ce0dacb8edd98d6be5caba

## Goal
Rewrite one short instructor-provided paragraph as a two-sentence summary tuned to one named reader: an executive (who needs risk posture and a decision ask) or a citizen (who needs to know what changes for them and what does not). You walk away with your reader label plus two sentences posted as `READER: ___ | SUMMARY: ___`.

**Assets:** Short instructor-provided paragraph (see `board-items.tsv` for the reader/output table).

**🌐 MS Co-pilot Specific Info:** Paste the paragraph into Copilot with the reader named in the prompt (“for a deputy minister” / “for a resident with no policy background”) and compare its draft with yours, where did it over- or under-simplify?

**🧩 Pattern watch:** Design pattern, **Audience-first prompting**: name the reader in the prompt before asking for the summary. Anti-pattern, **One-size summary**: the same paragraph for the CIO and the resident.

> ℹ️ **Good to know:** The Plain Writing Act of 2010 legally requires US federal agencies to communicate so the intended audience understands on the first read. plainlanguage.gov is the official home of that standard. Your Executive-versus-Citizen rewrite is exactly the skill the law assumes. (Source: [plainlanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/law/))

## Run steps 🪜
1. Read the source paragraph and list its facts: Q3 timing, routine inquiries only, benefits decisions excluded, human review on escalations.
2. Pick your named reader from the table in `board-items.tsv`: **Executive** (two sentences: risk posture and decision ask) or **Citizen** (two sentences: what changes for me and what does not).
3. Write exactly two sentences for that reader: executives get the guardrails and what you need decided; citizens get what changes in their experience and what stays human.
4. Post in Zoom chat as `READER: ___ | SUMMARY: ___` (or a Mural sticky in the same format if you are in the room).
5. Read a post from someone who chose the *other* reader: name one fact they kept that you cut, and one they cut that you kept.
6. If the instructor runs it with AI, ask your approved assistant for the same two-reader summaries and note in one chat reply where naming the reader changed its output.

## Key takeaway 💡
Naming the reader before you summarize, in your own head or in a prompt, is what turns a generic summary into a useful one: same facts, different decisions about what to keep.

## Common mistake to name ⚠️
Writing a long, reader-less summary with no stated audience or required format: "summarize this" thinking.

## If finished early ⏩
Rewrite your summary for the opposite reader (executive ↔ citizen) without changing a single fact, feel which words have to move, and note the hardest swap in one chat line.

## ⭐ Bonus (optional)
Do a third pass for a new reader, agency counsel, in two sentences: what is legally safe about this pilot and what needs review before launch. Post `READER: COUNSEL | SUMMARY: ...` and notice how the same paragraph has now produced three different documents.
