# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
Scoring against the 0–3 rubric instead of gut feel is what makes moderation defensible and consistent across reviewers. The rubric forces a distinction people naturally blur: rude (1) is about tone, abusive (2) is about targeting a person, and threatening (3) is about implied harm, and conflating them is how borderline calls change with the reviewer. Marking the most context-dependent phrase is not an admission that the rubric failed, it is the lesson itself, because automated scorers face exactly the same ambiguity without the human context to resolve it, which is why every tool needs a human escalation path. The escalation rule for the top score is the part that must survive disagreement: a possible threat gets escalated even when intent is disputed, because the downside of being wrong is a person's safety.

## A complete solution
The full placement, in the remote capture format: `P1=2, P2=1, P3=1, P4=3`. P1 "You are stupid" scores 2, a direct attack on a person, which is the definition of abusive on this rubric. P2 "This relationship sucks" scores 1, rude but aimed at a situation, not targeted harassment by itself. P3 "You are acting like a jerk" scores 1, aimed at behavior rather than identity, though context and repetition could push it up. P4 "I know where you live" scores 3, threatening, with escalation.

P4 is the one to defend out loud. The wrong instinct is "no profanity, no insult, so 0 or 1," and that instinct is exactly why keyword moderation fails: the words are polite and the payload is menace. The correct call is that the phrase implies physical reach over the target, which is what 3 means on this rubric, and it gets escalated to a human or safety process even if it was meant as a joke, because joking intent cannot be verified from the text alone. The context comment to leave on the board: "P4 depends most on context, between close friends joking about carpooling it could be a 0, but from a stranger in a complaint thread it is a hard 3 with escalation; since the scorer cannot see the relationship, the safe default is 3 plus human review." P1 is the runner-up disagreement worth hearing: among friends trading banter it can honestly drop to 1, but against an unknown target the default stays 2.
