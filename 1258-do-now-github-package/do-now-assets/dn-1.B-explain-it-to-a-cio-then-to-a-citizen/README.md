# Do Now 1.B: Explain It to a CIO, Then to a Citizen 🎯

**Time:** 5 minutes
**Format:** No sandbox or tooling required, this activity is done mentally and captured in Zoom chat (or on the Mural board if you are in the room).
**Mural:** https://app.mural.co/t/day10626/m/day10626/1790240784145/1d6c3055140c3f3d85f9ef06e435ebefc23eba24

## Goal
Take the concept the instructor names, generative AI or classic ML, and explain it twice in the capture channel: one sentence for a CIO, one sentence for a citizen, both accurate and nothing alike. You are practicing audience switching, the most-used communication skill in government AI work.

**Assets:** One course concept chosen by the instructor (see `board-items.tsv` for the CIO/citizen cues).

**🌐 MS Co-pilot Specific Info:** Draft both versions in Copilot, then edit them yourself, Copilot is good at audience switching, but the skill you are practicing is judging whether each version is right for its reader.

## Run steps 🪜
1. Note the concept the instructor names, generative AI or classic ML.
2. Draft your CIO sentence: lead with what it means for the agency, efficiency, risk controls, measurable service outcomes, in one sentence.
3. Draft your citizen sentence: plain words, no acronyms, for classic ML, "a pattern-spotter that scores or classifies cases" rather than "a supervised model".
4. Check the pair against the cues in `board-items.tsv`: does the citizen version keep the honest caveat that people still check the output?
5. Post both sentences together in Zoom chat, labeled `CIO:` and `CITIZEN:`, or as one Mural sticky if you are in the room.
6. Read three other pairs, reply to one where the citizen sentence still hides jargon, and be ready to read your pair aloud.

## Key takeaway 💡
The same concept must survive two audiences: a CIO version that speaks in outcomes, cost, and risk, and a citizen version in plain words with an honest caveat, if either one is wrong, the explanation is wrong.

## Study further 📚
- [PlainLanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/), the U.S. government's official site for writing so the public understands.
- [Federal plain language guidelines, PlainLanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/guidelines/), the concrete rules behind the citizen version of your sentence.
- [Plain language, Digital.gov](https://digital.gov/topics/plain-language/), resources and community for plain-language writing in government digital services.

## Common mistake to name ⚠️
Writing one sentence and calling it both, a CIO sentence full of "leverage" and "transform" is jargon to a citizen, and a citizen sentence with no caveat about human checking is misleading to a CIO.

## If finished early ⏩
Write the third audience: one sentence for a front-line staff member who will actually use the tool, focused on what changes in their Tuesday workflow.
