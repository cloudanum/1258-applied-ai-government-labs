# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
Doing the full multiplication chain, not quoting a per-request price, is what makes this defensible. At agency scale, cost is users × requests/day × tokens × working days, so every volume assumption moves the bill linearly and there is no shortcut around the arithmetic. The second move that earns the point is separating input from output tokens: output tokens usually cost several times more, so the 400 output tokens per request punch far above their share of the 1,600-token total. The weakest answer, "it costs pennies per request, so it is cheap," fails because it never multiplies, and the second-weakest, "users are the biggest driver," fails because doubling any volume lever has the identical linear effect, while per-token prices across model tiers differ by an order of magnitude or more. Sensitivity, not the point estimate, is the skill: the driver you name must be the one assumption that moves the total most.

## A complete solution
The chain: 12,000 staff × 2 requests/day = 24,000 requests/day. Tokens/day = 24,000 × 1,600 = 38.4M. Monthly tokens = 38.4M × 22 ≈ 845M, split into 633.6M input tokens (24,000 × 1,200 × 22) and 211.2M output tokens (24,000 × 400 × 22). Pricing with a mid-tier card of $0.40 per million input and $1.60 per million output: (633.6 × $0.40) + (211.2 × $1.60) ≈ $253 + $338 ≈ $590 per month. The same workload on a frontier card of $2.50 in / $10.00 out lands near $3,700 per month. Note the shape: output tokens are a quarter of the volume but roughly 57% of the mid-tier bill, and swapping model tiers moves the total about 6x here, with no change in usage at all.

Testing the levers one at a time: doubling requests per user doubles the bill, doubling tokens per request doubles the bill, and routing half of all requests to a smaller model at one-tenth the price drops the bill to 55% of its original size, a 45% saving, while touching only the model-choice assumption. That is why the strongest chat post names model choice, not a volume lever. A complete post reads: "biggest cost driver: model choice because at 845M tokens/month (12,000 users × 2 requests × 1,600 tokens × 22 days) every volume lever moves the bill only linearly, while per-token prices across model tiers differ by 10x or more; routing half our 24,000 daily requests to a small model at one-tenth the price cuts the monthly bill about 45% with no reduction in service."

If a colleague posts "biggest cost driver: users," the reconciliation reply is the assumption you would both need to agree on: whether request volume could plausibly double. If usage is capped by policy, volume levers are bounded and the model tier remains the only lever with an order-of-magnitude swing, which settles the disagreement before the debrief.
