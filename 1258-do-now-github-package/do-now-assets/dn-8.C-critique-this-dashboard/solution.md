# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
Checking the axis, the denominator, and the definition before believing the trend is what makes the critique defensible, because every dashboard is an argument and this one argues harder than its data supports. The truncated y-axis is the classic tell: starting at 80 instead of 0 turns a real 19% improvement into what looks like a fivefold leap, and the headline "Citizen Service Resolution Rate Soars!" leans on exactly that distortion, even though the trend quietly dips from 85 in February to 84 in March. The weaker instinct is to critique colors and layout, which feels productive but leaves the three failures that matter, scale, denominator, and definition, untouched. The denominator critique deserves the vote because it is the one the chart cannot survive: 98 resolved in June is success if 100 requests arrived and a crisis if 300 did, and no axis fix can rescue a number whose meaning depends on data that is not shown.

## A complete solution
Three red stickies and one green. Red, misleading: the y-axis starts at 80, so the climb from 82 in January to 98 in June fills the chart like a rocket; on a zero-based axis the same bars would show a modest, honest rise, and the "Soars!" headline would have to earn its word against a trend that actually dipped in March. Red, missing: the denominator, resolved out of how many received? With no requests-received count, no backlog, no channel or geography breakdown, and no uncertainty interval, the chart cannot answer the only question a service dashboard exists for: are we keeping up? Red, unclear: "resolved" is undefined. Closed ticket, satisfied citizen, and SLA met are three different numbers wearing one label, and until the definition is pinned the trend is uninterpretable. A side observation worth a comment: the category pie adds little, every slice sits between 13% and 15%, so it confirms requests are spread evenly but shows no trend and no priority.

Green sticky: the month-over-month resolution trend across six months is real signal worth keeping. Once the axis starts at 0 and a denominator appears, this exact panel becomes the useful core of the dashboard, which is why the critique targets what is hidden rather than declaring the whole board worthless.

The vote lands on the missing denominator, matching the sample's own caption hint. The chat fallback post reads: "worst issue: no denominator, we cannot tell whether 98 resolved is keeping up with demand | one fix: plot requests received next to resolved each month, or show resolved as a percent of received, on a zero-based axis." If a colleague votes for the truncated axis instead, the defense is priority: the axis exaggerates a trend that exists, while the missing denominator conceals whether the trend is good news at all, and a hidden crisis outranks an exaggerated success.
