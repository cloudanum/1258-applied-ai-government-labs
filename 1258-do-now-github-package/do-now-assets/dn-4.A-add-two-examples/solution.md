# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
Two examples replace interpretation with imitation: the model stops guessing what "good" means and starts copying it, and holding the facts identical between EX1 and EX2 while changing only the audience proves that audience alone drives vocabulary, framing, and length. One input/output pair settles tone, length, and structure at once; a paragraph of adjectives leaves every word open to the model's own reading.

## A complete solution
Post the pair to Zoom chat: `EX1 input: outage window + impacted service -> output: short resident email with status and next update time` and `EX2 input: same facts -> output: version for a non-technical council member`.

EX1, input facts: water main break on Bank St, service outage 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. on January 5, drinking water shutoff for the 200–400 blocks. Output, the short resident email: "Subject: Water shutoff on Bank St today, next update at 2:00 p.m. A water main break has shut off drinking water service to the 200–400 blocks of Bank St. Crews are on site and service is expected to be restored by 6:00 p.m. today, January 5. The city will post the next status update at 2:00 p.m., and sooner if the timeline changes." That output pins down what W1 was missing: subject line, status first, clock times, plain words, four sentences.

EX2, same facts, output for a non-technical council member: "For your ward bulletin: about 200 households near Bank St lost water service this morning after a water main break. Repairs are underway and we expect service back by 6:00 p.m. today. Your office can tell callers that the next city update comes at 2:00 p.m., and that bottled water pickup will be available at the community centre if the outage runs past 6:00 p.m." Same facts, but the framing shifts to households affected and what the member's office can tell callers, with no operational detail.
