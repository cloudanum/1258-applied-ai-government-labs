# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
Two examples work because they replace interpretation with imitation: the model stops guessing what "good" means and starts copying what good looks like. Holding the facts identical between EX1 and EX2 while changing only the audience is the real teaching move, because it proves that audience alone drives vocabulary, framing, and length. A paragraph of adjectives like "professional, concise, empathetic" leaves every one of those words open to the model's own reading, while one input/output pair settles tone, length, and structure at once. The weaker instinct is to stack more instructions on W1; the failure mode this avoids is a draft that is fluent but wrong for the reader, which in government means confused residents calling the ward office or a council member repeating operational detail that was never cleared for release. Examples also make your intent inspectable: a colleague can correct your example in chat before the model ever sees it.

## A complete solution
Post the pair to Zoom chat in the README's format: `EX1 input: outage window + impacted service -> output: short resident email with status and next update time` and `EX2 input: same facts -> output: version for a non-technical council member`, with the actual content behind each line.

EX1, input facts: water main break on Bank St, service outage 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. on January 5, impacted service is the drinking water shutoff for the 200–400 blocks. Output, the short resident email: "Subject: Water shutoff on Bank St today, next update at 2:00 p.m. A water main break has shut off drinking water service to the 200–400 blocks of Bank St. Crews are on site and service is expected to be restored by 6:00 p.m. today, January 5. The city will post the next status update at 2:00 p.m., and sooner if the timeline changes." That output pins down everything W1 was missing: subject line, status first, clock times, plain words, four sentences.

EX2, same facts, output for a non-technical council member: "For your ward bulletin: about 200 households near Bank St lost water service this morning after a water main break. Repairs are underway and we expect service back by 6:00 p.m. today. Your office can tell callers that the next city update comes at 2:00 p.m., and that bottled water pickup will be available at the community centre if the outage runs past 6:00 p.m." Same facts, but the framing shifts to households affected, what the member's office can say to callers, and constituent-facing actions, with no operational detail like valve closures. Read together, the pair teaches the model that "the outage email" is not one thing: audience changes the output, and the example is what tells it how.
