# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
You cannot govern what you have not listed, and the risk lives in the pairing, not the tool: a personal, unmanaged account touching internal or case data is almost always the worst entry. Keeping the inventory blame-free is functional, not soft; punished listings just go quieter and the inventory stays empty.

## A complete solution
A complete three-sticky mini-inventory, in the chat fallback format `tool + data type + risk`:

1. `personal chatbot account + meeting notes (internal deliberative) = biggest risk`
2. `free online summarizer + public web text = low risk, still unmanaged`
3. `personal-account transcription bot + internal team call recordings = high risk, calls contain personal and deliberative content`

Sticky 1 gets the color tag: a personal account has no agency agreement behind it, and deliberative meeting notes must never leave managed systems. Sticky 2 stays on the board because unmanaged-but-harmless tools are where the sanctioned-alternative conversation starts. Approved alternative, added as a comment on sticky 1: "use the licensed Copilot tenant for meeting notes instead."

Board-scan finding for the debrief: if half the room lists a transcription bot and nobody can name who cleared it, that gap is the policy conversation to take back. The three stickies are also the first rows of a formal register: tool, data type, risk, approved alternative.
