# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
You cannot govern what you have not listed, and every formal AI inventory starts from exactly this move: naming the tools in use and the data that flows into each. The risk lives in the pairing, not the tool alone: a personal, unmanaged account touching internal or case data is almost always the worst entry, because the content leaves every agreement and safeguard the agency controls, while the same kind of tool on public web text is low risk. Keeping the exercise blame-free is functional, not soft: if listing a tool invites punishment, the usage does not stop, it just goes quieter, and the inventory stays empty. A weaker inventory that lists tools without data types hides the entire risk picture, because "someone uses a transcription bot" is harmless or alarming depending entirely on what it records.

## A complete solution
A complete three-sticky mini-inventory, written in the chat fallback format `tool + data type + risk`:

1. `personal chatbot account + meeting notes (internal deliberative) = biggest risk`
2. `free online summarizer + public web text = low risk, still unmanaged`
3. `personal-account transcription bot + internal team call recordings = high risk, calls contain personal and deliberative content`

Sticky 1 gets the color tag as the biggest risk: a personal account has no agency agreement behind it, and deliberative meeting notes are precisely the content class that must never leave managed systems. Sticky 2 stays on the board even though its risk is low, because unmanaged-but-harmless tools are where the sanctioned-alternative conversation starts. The one-line approved alternative, added as a comment on sticky 1: "use the licensed Copilot tenant for meeting notes instead," since every inventory item deserves a sanctioned path rather than a bare prohibition.

The board-scan finding to bring to the debrief: if half the room lists a transcription bot and nobody can name who cleared it, that gap is the policy conversation to take back to the agency. And the three stickies are already the first rows of a formal register with the four columns tool, data type, risk, approved alternative, which is how most real agency AI inventories actually began.
