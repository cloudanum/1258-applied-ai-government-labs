# Do Now 3.D: Shadow AI Inventory (Optional) 🎯

**Time:** 5 minutes
**Format:** No sandbox or tooling required — this activity is done mentally and captured directly on the Mural board with blank stickies (or in Zoom chat if you are remote).
**Mural:** https://app.mural.co/t/day10626/m/day10626/1790240794067/bbbcebcdc79046f7d0e730f21cc729837a32d54b

## Goal
By the end of this five-minute exercise you will have surfaced the unofficial "shadow AI" use already happening around you: three tools people actually reach for, the type of data that flows into each, and the single biggest risk on your list. The artifact is a mini-inventory on the Mural board — three stickies pairing a tool with its data type, one of them marked as highest risk. The deeper skill is the governance instinct behind every federal AI inventory: you cannot govern what you have not listed, and the riskiest entry is almost always a personal, unmanaged account touching internal or case data. You will leave able to see your own workplace the way an AI governance lead does — as a map of tools, data flows, and unpriced risk.

## Why it matters
Listing unofficial AI use already happening becomes defensible when learners must place, mark, or choose and then explain one decision — and the list itself is the prerequisite for every governance step that follows. An honest, blame-free inventory is what turns shadow AI from an invisible risk into a manageable one.

**Assets:** Blank stickies (see `board-items.tsv` for the starter and capture template).

**🌐 With Copilot in the browser:** Your inventory contrast: agency-licensed Copilot in the browser is the sanctioned path; anything reached through personal accounts or unapproved tools goes in the shadow-AI column, even when it feels identical to use.

## Run steps 🪜
1. Open the Mural board linked above and think of real, unofficial AI use you have seen or done — personal chatbot accounts, free summarizers, transcription bots, image generators, grammar tools with AI features.
2. Add three blank stickies, one tool per sticky — no colleague names or call-outs; this is an inventory, not an investigation.
3. Beside each tool sticky, add the data type that actually flows into it: public web text, meeting notes, internal deliberative drafts, or case details with personal information.
4. Mark the biggest risk with a color tag or comment — the pairing of a personal/unmanaged account with internal or case data (e.g. personal chatbot + meeting notes marked deliberative) is usually it.
5. Working remotely? Post the chat fallback in Zoom instead: `tool + data type + risk`, e.g. `personal chatbot + meeting notes (internal deliberative) = biggest risk`.
6. Scan the whole board before the debrief: find one tool everyone seems to use that nobody has cleared — that gap is the policy conversation to take back to your agency.

## Key takeaway 💡
Shadow AI is already here — governance starts with an honest inventory, and the riskiest entry on it is almost always a personal account touching internal data.

## Study further 📚
- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) — the U.S. reference framework whose Govern function begins with mapping and inventorying AI use.
- [What is shadow IT? — IBM Think](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/shadow-it) — why unmanaged technology use spreads, and why visibility beats punishment.
- [Artificial Intelligence: An Accountability Framework for Federal Agencies (GAO-21-519SP) — GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-21-519sp) — the federal accountability framework that expects agencies to document and oversee their AI use.

## Common mistake to name ⚠️
Treating shadow AI as a discipline problem instead of a visibility problem — if people fear punishment for listing a tool, the usage does not stop, it just goes quieter. Keep the inventory blame-free or it stays empty.

## If finished early ⏩
Draft the one-line approved alternative for your biggest-risk entry — e.g. "use the licensed Copilot tenant for meeting notes instead" — and add it as a comment; every inventory item deserves a sanctioned path.

## ⭐ Bonus (optional)
Turn your three stickies into the first rows of a formal register with four columns: tool, data type, risk, approved alternative. Bring it to your agency's AI governance or security contact — most real AI inventories started exactly this way.
