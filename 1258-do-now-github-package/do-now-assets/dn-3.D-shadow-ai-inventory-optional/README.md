# Do Now 3.D: Shadow AI Inventory (Optional) 🎯

**Time:** 5 minutes
**Format:** No sandbox or tooling required, this activity is done mentally and captured directly on the Mural board with blank stickies (or in Zoom chat if you are remote).
**Mural:** https://app.mural.co/t/day10626/m/day10626/1790240794067/bbbcebcdc79046f7d0e730f21cc729837a32d54b

## Goal
List three unofficial AI tools people around you actually use, the type of data that flows into each, and the biggest risk on your list. You walk away with a three-sticky mini-inventory on the Mural board, one entry marked highest risk.

**Assets:** Blank stickies (see `board-items.tsv` for the starter and capture template).

**🌐 MS Co-pilot Specific Info:** Your inventory contrast: agency-licensed Copilot in the browser is the sanctioned path; anything reached through personal accounts or unapproved tools goes in the shadow-AI column, even when it feels identical to use.

## Run steps 🪜
1. Open the Mural board and think of real, unofficial AI use you have seen or done: personal chatbot accounts, free summarizers, transcription bots, image generators, grammar tools with AI features.
2. Add three blank stickies, one tool per sticky, no colleague names; this is an inventory, not an investigation.
3. Beside each tool, add the data type that actually flows into it: public web text, meeting notes, internal deliberative drafts, or case details with personal information.
4. Mark the biggest risk with a color tag or comment; a personal account touching internal or case data is usually it.
5. Working remotely? Post `tool + data type + risk` in Zoom chat instead, e.g. `personal chatbot + meeting notes (internal deliberative) = biggest risk`.
6. Before the debrief, find one tool everyone uses that nobody has cleared; that gap is the policy conversation to take back to your agency.

## Key takeaway 💡
Shadow AI is already here, governance starts with an honest inventory, and the riskiest entry on it is almost always a personal account touching internal data.

## Study further 📚
- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), the U.S. reference framework whose Govern function begins with mapping and inventorying AI use.
- [What is shadow IT?, IBM Think](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/shadow-it), why unmanaged technology use spreads, and why visibility beats punishment.
- [Artificial Intelligence: An Accountability Framework for Federal Agencies (GAO-21-519SP), GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-21-519sp), the federal accountability framework that expects agencies to document and oversee their AI use.

## Common mistake to name ⚠️
Treating shadow AI as a discipline problem instead of a visibility problem; if listing a tool invites punishment, usage goes quieter and the inventory stays empty.

## If finished early ⏩
Draft the one-line approved alternative for your biggest-risk entry, e.g. "use the licensed Copilot tenant for meeting notes instead", and add it as a comment.

## ⭐ Bonus (optional)
Turn your three stickies into the first rows of a formal register: tool, data type, risk, approved alternative. Bring it to your agency's AI governance or security contact.
