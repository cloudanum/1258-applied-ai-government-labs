# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
A role line works because it decides what the model notices before it writes a word: a records officer scans for exemptions and deadlines, while a communications director scans for what a resident would misread and what a reporter would quote. The contract half matters just as much, because "issues, exemptions to check, and a draft response in 5 bullets" is reviewable and "summarize this" is not; you can say an answer is wrong only if you first said what right looks like. The failure mode this avoids is confident rambling from "You are an expert": fluent text with no named fields, no one accountable for what is missing, and no template you could hand to a colleague. In government work the role also sets the caution level, which is why role plus contract beats either half alone.

## A complete solution
For the records task, the chat post is: `Task: Summarize a public-records request | Role: You are a government records officer | Output must look like: issues identified, exemptions to check, and a draft response in 5 bullets`. For the chatbot task: `Task: Review a chatbot answer | Role: You are a cautious communications director | Output must look like: factual risk, tone risk, and a revised answer`.

The chatbot review is the one worth defending out loud, because the role choice is the tricky part. The wrong instinct is "You are an AI expert," but an AI expert flags how the model failed, and the person who owns the failure in government is the communications director, whose job is to ask what happens when this answer gets screenshotted. A complete defense: "I assign the cautious communications director because the risks are factual and reputational, not architectural. The contract forces the review into three fields I can act on: factual risk is anything the answer asserts that we cannot source, tone risk is anything dismissive, speculative, or blaming the resident, and the revised answer is the version I could publish today. If a classmate assigned 'AI safety engineer' instead, their review would flag injection and grounding gaps, which are real, but none of those tell me whether I can stand behind the sentence at a press conference." Both prompts fit the `Task | Role | Output must look like` template exactly, and either is specific enough that a colleague could run it tomorrow and get a comparable result.
