# Do Now 2.C: Ask for Datasets, Then Verify 🎯

**Time:** 5 minutes
**Format:** No sandbox or special tooling required, this activity can be done mentally (reasoning through what you would ask and how you would check it) and is captured in Zoom chat (in-room participants may add their result to the Mural board instead).
**Mural:** https://app.mural.co/t/day10626/m/day10626/1790240789049/0ff478b72a7508905c90a132dde3e48c04d101d6

## Goal
Use an approved AI assistant to suggest two real public datasets on a topic your agency cares about, then verify that at least one actually exists by opening the URL and confirming it lands on a live, current dataset page. You walk away with one verified link posted as `VERIFIED: <url>`, not a list of AI suggestions.

**Assets:** Approved AI assistant + web (see `board-items.tsv` for the starter and capture template).

**🌐 MS Co-pilot Specific Info:** Copilot will happily suggest dataset names and URLs, that is exactly the trap this exercise trains: never repost a link you have not opened yourself in the same browser.

**🧩 Pattern watch:** Design pattern, **Verified retrieval**: treat every AI-suggested dataset as a lead, not a link to repost. Anti-pattern, **Confident fabrication accepted**: reposting a dataset URL that was never opened.

> ℹ️ **Good to know:** Data.gov launched in May 2009 as the flagship of the US open government effort. It now indexes hundreds of thousands of datasets from federal, state, and local agencies. Even here the verify-first habit applies: open the dataset page, not just the search snippet. (Source: [Data.gov](https://www.data.gov/about/))

## Run steps 🪜
1. Pick a real question your agency works on, flooding, wait times, air quality, service requests, so the result is genuinely useful.
2. Ask the approved AI assistant: "Find two public datasets about <topic> and give me the exact source URLs."
3. Open each suggested URL and check three things: does the page load, is it actually a dataset or metadata page, and is it current?
4. Discard anything that 404s, redirects to a generic homepage, or is not what was promised; that is a hallucinated or stale link.
5. Post only the confirmed link in Zoom chat as `VERIFIED: <url>` plus a few words on what the dataset is (or a Mural sticky in the same format if you are in the room).
6. Open one classmate's `VERIFIED:` post, run your three checks on it, and reply with what you found.

## Key takeaway 💡
An AI assistant is a discovery engine, not an authority, the only dataset link worth sharing is one you opened and checked yourself.

## Study further 📚
- [Data.gov](https://www.data.gov), the U.S. federal open-data catalog; the first place to check whether a suggested dataset really exists.
- [Open Government Portal, Government of Canada](https://open.canada.ca/en), Canada's official open-data and open-information search, a reliable verification target.
- [What are AI hallucinations?, IBM Think](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-hallucinations), why language models invent convincing but fake links and citations.

## Common mistake to name ⚠️
Posting the AI's long, confident answer with no verified source: a list of three suggested links where zero were opened.

## If finished early ⏩
Verify the second dataset as well, or tighten your prompt with a constraint, "only .gov or .gc.ca sources updated in the last two years", and note whether the suggestions improve.

## ⭐ Bonus (optional)
Repeat the hunt without AI: search data.gov or open.canada.ca directly for the same topic and compare the two routes. Post one chat line on which found the better dataset faster and how much verification time the AI route cost you.
