# Do Now 9.B: Pick Your First Use Case 🎯

**Time:** 5 minutes
**Format:** No sandbox or tooling required, this activity is done mentally and captured in Zoom chat (or on the Mural board if you are in the room).
**Mural:** https://app.mural.co/t/day10626/m/day10626/1790240817117/bb9b1cd4420250048e9cc4b1eec9ec1306e7a9e1

## Goal
By the end of this five-minute warm-up you will have named one realistic first AI use case for your own agency, its primary user, and one measurable result, and posted all three as a single structured message. The worked example on the board shows the shape: "Draft first-pass responses to routine permit status questions" for "permit intake staff," with "median first-response time drops from 2 days to same day with no increase in corrections." Your artifact is that three-part answer in Zoom chat, or a three-part sticky on the Mural board if you are in the room. The instinct this builds is scope discipline: first projects should be routine, low-risk, and measurable enough to defend to leadership in one sentence.

## Why it matters
Choosing the first use case is where most agency AI efforts succeed or stall, too big and the project dies in review, too vague and it can never be measured. Doing it in chat makes the reasoning visible and easy to correct before anyone writes a proposal.

**Assets:** Your agency context, plus the worked permit-intake example (see `board-items.tsv`).

**🌐 With Copilot in the browser:** A strong first use case for most teams is shaped exactly like Copilot's strengths: drafting, summarizing, and triaging text your staff already produce, with a human reviewing before anything leaves the building.

## Run steps 🪜
1. Think of one repetitive, text-heavy task in your own agency context, something staff do many times a week with a predictable shape.
2. Write the use case in one plain sentence, following the worked example: "Draft first-pass responses to routine permit status questions."
3. Name the primary user who would touch the tool daily, e.g. "permit intake staff," not "the agency."
4. Name one measurable result with a baseline and a target, e.g. "median first-response time drops from 2 days to same day with no increase in corrections."
5. Post all three in Zoom chat as one structured message: `Use case: ... | User: ... | Measurable result: ...`. In the room, write the same three parts on a Mural sticky and place it on the board.
6. Read two other posts in chat or on the board and reply to one, naming in a single line why it is, or is not, a good first use case.

## Key takeaway 💡
A good first AI use case is boring on purpose, routine, low-risk, with a named user and a measurable result you could defend to a CIO in one sentence.

## Study further 📚
- [AI use cases, IBM Think](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-use-cases), how organizations frame and categorize practical AI use cases.
- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), the U.S. government reference for scoping and governing AI systems before deployment.
- [Artificial Intelligence: An Accountability Framework for Federal Agencies (GAO-21-519SP)](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-21-519sp), the federal framework for selecting and overseeing agency AI use cases.

## Common mistake to name ⚠️
Picking a flashy moonshot with no named user and no metric. Require one named user and one measurable result with a baseline before accepting any answer.

## If finished early ⏩
Improve one answer, yours or a colleague's, by adding a constraint: audience, output format, source limit, or refusal rule.

## ⭐ Bonus (optional)
Stress-test your use case against two filters in one chat line each: is the data for it actually available, and would a wrong answer harm a resident? If either answer worries you, say what you would shrink first.
