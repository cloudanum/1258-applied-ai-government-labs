# Do Now 5.A: Rank These Risks 🎯

**Time:** 5 minutes
**Format:** No special tooling or environment required — this activity is done mentally and captured directly on the Mural board (or in Zoom chat if you are remote).
**Mural:** https://app.mural.co/t/day10626/m/day10626/1790240799056/a4b3b37ad0e4cf67f7ff02483c02f633f8dfb96d

## Goal
By the end of this five-minute warm-up you will have scanned eight AI risk stickies (R1–R8) — from data poisoning and prompt injection to insider misuse and bias failures — placed them on the 3×3 likelihood/impact grid, and dragged the two you would fund first into the FUND FIRST zone. The artifact you produce is a ranked board, one written defense of a pick, and one respectful disagreement with a colleague's ranking. The instinct you build is that risk ranking is a funding decision, not a fear contest: likelihood and impact in your own agency's context beat headline anxiety, and every pick should survive a facilitator question like "who controls training data and labels?" (R1) or "what content reaches the prompt?" (R3).

## Why it matters
No agency can mitigate all eight risks at once, so the two you fund first quietly define your real security posture — and oversight will ask why you chose them. Ranking becomes defensible when learners must place, mark, or choose and then explain one decision in their own agency context.

**Assets:** Risk stickies R1–R8 plus the 3×3 likelihood/impact grid (`risk-grid.png`).

## Run steps 🪜
1. Open the Mural board and scan all eight risk stickies R1–R8 slowly — note which ones target the training data (R1, R8), the inputs (R2, R3), the model or service itself (R4, R5, R6), or the people running it (R7).
2. For each sticky, silently rate likelihood and impact for YOUR agency — not for the headlines.
3. Drag each sticky onto the 3×3 grid (see `risk-grid.png` for the axes): high/low likelihood against high/low impact.
4. Drag your top two risks into the FUND FIRST zone.
5. Add a comment on one FUND FIRST pick naming both its likelihood and its impact in one sentence — e.g. R3 prompt injection: "every submitted document reaches the prompt, so likelihood is high, and hijacked instructions make impact high."
6. Find a colleague's ranking you disagree with and add one disagreement comment — use the facilitator question on that sticky (for R1: "who controls training data and labels?").
7. Remote? Post your ranking in Zoom chat as `FUND FIRST: R3, R7 — because ___` and reply to one ranking you disagree with.

## Key takeaway 💡
Risk ranking is a funding decision, not a fear contest — the two risks you fund first should have the highest likelihood and impact in your own agency's context, and you should be able to defend each pick in one sentence.

## Study further 📚
- [Adversarial Machine Learning taxonomy (NIST AI 100-2e2023) — NIST CSRC](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ai/100/2/e2023/final) — the federal taxonomy behind risks like poisoning, evasion, extraction, and inference.
- [OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications — OWASP](https://owasp.org/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications/) — the community-ranked list of LLM risks, including prompt injection.
- [MITRE ATLAS — MITRE](https://atlas.mitre.org/) — real-world tactics and case studies of attacks on AI-enabled systems.

## Common mistake to name ⚠️
Ranking by fear headlines instead of likelihood and impact in your own agency context — require every FUND FIRST pick to name both before it counts.

## If finished early ⏩
Choose your top risk and name the first metric that would tell you it is getting worse — for prompt injection (R3), for example, the share of submitted documents containing instruction-like text.
