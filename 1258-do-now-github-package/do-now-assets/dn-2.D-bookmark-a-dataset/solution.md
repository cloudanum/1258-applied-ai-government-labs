# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
The artifact is private by design: a bookmark in your own browser plus one line in your own notes, with only the theme shared aloud. That privacy is what lets people pick datasets genuinely tied to their work without exposing sensitive program context, and the theme-only roundup still lets the room compare categories. Bookmarking the landing page rather than a deep file link is the durable choice: deep links break on refresh cycles, while the landing page carries the documentation and update history you need to re-judge the source next month. And judging by the three tests (real downloadable data, a data dictionary or documentation, a recent update date) instead of by title is what separates a reusable source from a good-sounding search result. A dataset you cannot find again in March is worth nothing, which is why one saved, named, verified source beats ten interesting ones left in a search tab.

## A complete solution
A complete sample, on the topic of air quality for a regional program office:

- Bookmarked page: `https://www.epa.gov/outdoor-air-quality-data`, the EPA outdoor air quality landing page, chosen over a direct CSV link because it holds the download formats, documentation, and update history in one place.
- Bookmark name: "EPA county air quality (annual refresh)", named so that future-me knows both what it is and how fresh to expect it to be.
- Three-test check before saving: real downloadable data (yes, CSV extracts by county), a data dictionary and documentation (yes), and a recent update date (confirmed on the page).
- Private line in my own notes, never posted: "This lets me compare county-level air quality trends across our service region without standing up new collection, and the documentation is strong enough to cite in a briefing."
- Theme line if the instructor calls a roundup, posted to the Mural board or Zoom chat: "found a county-level air quality source." No program names, no case details, no internal systems.

The two failure modes this avoids are worth naming in debrief: bookmarking a bare CSV link that 404s after the next annual refresh, and sharing the full justification line publicly when it happens to mention an internal program or case type. The bookmark name and the theme sentence are the two habits to keep: both are written for a reader who is not present-tense you.
