# Sample / solution

## Why this approach works
The artifact is private by design, so people can pick datasets genuinely tied to their work without exposing program context. Bookmarking the landing page rather than a deep file link, and judging by the three tests instead of the title, is what makes the source findable and re-checkable next month.

## A complete solution
A complete sample, on the topic of air quality for a regional program office:

- Bookmarked page: `https://www.epa.gov/outdoor-air-quality-data`, the EPA outdoor air quality landing page, chosen over a direct CSV link because it holds the download formats, documentation, and update history in one place.
- Bookmark name: "EPA county air quality (annual refresh)", so future-me knows what it is and how fresh to expect it to be.
- Three-test check before saving: real downloadable data (CSV extracts by county), a data dictionary and documentation, and a recent update date confirmed on the page.
- Private line in my own notes, never posted: "This lets me compare county-level air quality trends across our service region without standing up new collection, and the documentation is strong enough to cite in a briefing."
- Theme line if the instructor calls a roundup: "found a county-level air quality source." No program names, no case details, no internal systems.

The two failure modes this avoids: a bare CSV link that 404s after the next annual refresh, and a justification line shared publicly when it mentions an internal program or case type.
